Hey, it's Erika. Welcome back to Erika Explains.

So, I moved from Chicago to Atlanta over twenty-seven years ago. And today I want to tell you what I wish somebody had told me before I picked up and headed south. Because if you're thinking about moving here, these are the things that actually decide whether you love it... or spend your first year wondering why you came. So let me explain.

First thing. Atlanta is not just one city.

When people say "Atlanta," they usually mean the whole region. And the region is big. The metro area that the federal government measures, it's officially called Atlanta, Sandy Springs, Roswell, is twenty-nine counties. Twenty-nine. Each one with its own government.

It's all Atlanta. It's connected... but it's kind of disconnected. Your commute, your social life, your schools, your home price, even how often you actually go into the city, all of that can depend on the neighborhood or the suburb you choose.

Schools are the clearest example. In most of Metro Atlanta, your school district follows the county line. But some cities run their own. The City of Atlanta has Atlanta Public Schools, and Decatur, Marietta and Buford each have their own city school systems too. So two homes a few minutes apart can be in two different districts. Check the actual address, not just the zip code. The state publishes report cards for every school, so you can look up the numbers yourself. And if you've got kids, the State of Georgia's advice is to start the enrollment process as early as you can.

Property taxes work the same way, county by county. I explain homestead exemptions in another episode, it's on my website, so listen to that one before you buy.

Okay. Second thing. Traffic.

Atlanta traffic is real. But distance is not the only issue. Ten miles can take fifteen minutes... or an hour.

The Census Bureau says the average commute here is about thirty-two minutes each way. But honestly, the average doesn't tell you much. Your route tells you everything.

And transit can help, in the right places. MARTA, our rail system, serves Fulton, DeKalb and Clayton counties. So if you're picturing yourself riding the train to work, make sure the train actually comes where you're planning to live.

So here's my advice. Choose your home based on how you actually plan to live. Before you fall in love with a house, drive the route you'll really drive. On a weekday. At the time you'd really leave.

Third thing. And nobody properly warned me about this one. The pollen.

The weather here is beautiful. But Atlanta turns yellow every spring. And if you never had allergies before, you might find you have them once you move here.

Atlanta's official pollen count comes from Atlanta Allergy and Asthma. They're the only counting station in the area certified by the National Allergy Bureau. For tree pollen, anything from fifteen hundred up is considered extremely high. And on March twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-five, the count hit fourteen thousand, eight hundred and one. That's a record.

So if you're moving with someone who has allergies or asthma, plan for spring before it gets here.

Now here's the important part. And it's the thing I wish I had known most.

Atlanta is an entrepreneurial city. Atlanta gave me room to grow professionally, build a business, raise my family, and create a completely different lifestyle. So after twenty-seven years, I can honestly say moving here changed my life. But choosing the right area matters.

Alright. Let's get practical, because Georgia gives new residents a pretty short clock on a few things.

Your driver's license. You've got thirty days from when you become a Georgia resident. If your old license is still valid, you'll turn it in and pass a vision test. And bring two proofs of your Georgia address, from two separate places.

Then your car. Also thirty days from when you move. You register it at your county tag office, but you need that Georgia license first. And here's the one that surprises people. New residents pay a one-time title tax, three percent of the car's fair market value. So budget for it.

The good news? Voting takes care of itself. Georgia registers you automatically when you apply for your license.

And if you buy a home, remember the homestead exemption. If you own it and live in it on January first, file by April first.
