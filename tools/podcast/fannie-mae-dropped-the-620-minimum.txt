Let me explain.

First, what changed. Fannie Mae announced it on November fifth, twenty twenty-five. And right now, the Selling Guide says it plainly. A minimum credit score is not required for loans that go through D U.

So what's D U? It's Desktop Underwriter. That's Fannie Mae's automated underwriting system. And instead of one cutoff number, D U looks at your whole file. In the guide's own words, credit scores are not an integral part of D U's risk assessment, because D U does its own analysis of your credit report.

And here's why that matters. I've seen clients with all the assets in the world struggle just because of credit score. One number used to close the door before anybody looked at the rest of the story.

Now here's the important part. No minimum score is not the same as no standards. This is not a free pass.

A few things did not change at all. Your scores still get pulled. Lenders still have to request scores from all three credit bureaus.

If a loan is underwritten by hand, instead of through D U, there's still a floor. Six twenty for a fixed-rate loan. Six forty for an adjustable-rate one.

Government-backed loans are their own thing, too. For F H A, V A and U S D A loans that Fannie Mae buys, the guide still lists a six twenty minimum.

And remember... Fannie Mae doesn't lend you the money. It buys mortgages from lenders. Your lender still makes the loan. So this change doesn't guarantee anybody a loan.

So if the score isn't the gatekeeper anymore... what is? The rest of your credit story. And Fannie Mae actually publishes what D U looks at.

Time is on your side. An older, established history helps. But a newer history isn't automatically considered high risk, especially when you've paid as agreed.

Recent late payments weigh more than old ones. The guide's own example is a thirty-day late payment from less than three months ago. That's a higher risk than one from several years back.

Keep your card balances low. Using your credit cards lightly, or paying them off regularly, is considered lower risk.

Medical collections? They're not used in D U's assessment at all.

And shopping for a mortgage is fine. When different mortgage lenders check your credit in the same time frame, D U doesn't treat that as a pile of separate inquiries. Applying for a lot of new credit, though... that's a different story.

And it's not only credit. D U also looks at your down payment, your savings, your debt compared to your income, the property, and the loan itself.

Now let's talk about rent. In my video I said that if you've got solid rental history, you might qualify even if your score isn't picture perfect. So here's how that actually works.

D U can use your rent payments to help your credit assessment. Only to help, never to hurt. So if you rent... pay it from a bank account in your name. The same way, every month. That paper trail is working for you.
